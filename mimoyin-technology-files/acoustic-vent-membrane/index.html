Waterproof Acoustic Vent Membrane for Microphone & Speaker (IP67/IP68)

What Is an Acoustic Vent Membrane? (透声膜 vs 透音膜)

In Chinese the product is called both 透声膜 ("sound-transmitting film") and 透音膜 ("sound-permeable film"). They refer to the same category: a membrane that transmits sound while staying waterproof. On this page we use the English term acoustic vent membrane / sound-permeable membrane to cover both. The membrane sits over the acoustic port of a mic or speaker, so the device can "hear" and "speak" through a waterproof barrier.

How an Acoustic Vent Membrane Works

The microporous structure passes sound waves (air pressure fluctuations) but stops liquid water and particles. ePTFE grades achieve acoustic loss of ≤ 1 dB—effectively transparent to sound—while TPU runs 6–10 dB and premium PU 0.5–1.5 dB*. Lower loss means clearer call quality and richer music.

Key Specifications

  • Acoustic loss: ≤ 1 dB (ePTFE), the metric that decides audio clarity.
  • Waterproof rating: IP67/IP68 validated, up to 10 m for ePTFE.
  • Air permeability: ml/min @ Ø10 mm orifice, supports pressure equalization behind the port.
  • Temperature range: -40°C to +260°C (ePTFE).

How to Select an Acoustic Vent Membrane

  1. Set the acoustic budget: if call clarity matters, choose ePTFE (≤1 dB).
  2. Confirm waterproof grade: IP67 for splashes, IP68 for submersion.
  3. Match the port shape: die-cut to the exact acoustic opening.
  4. Pick install method: self-adhesive, heat-stake, or weld.

Insertion Loss Measurement Data

Acoustic transparency is quantified as insertion loss at 1 kHz, measured on the assembled device (IEC 60268 method). Lower is better. Mimoyin ePTFE acoustic vents are specified at ≤ 1 dB.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will the acoustic vent membrane muffle sound?

ePTFE versions add ≤ 1 dB loss—imperceptible. TPU/PU add more; choose by audio budget.

What is the difference between 透声膜 and 透音膜?

None in function—both mean a waterproof sound-transmitting membrane. 透声 emphasizes "sound passes", 透音 emphasizes "sound permeates"; English uses acoustic vent / sound-permeable membrane for both.

Can the membrane handle repeated washing or sweat?

Yes. ePTFE is hydrophobic and survives sweat, rain, and routine cleaning per IP ratings.

Does it affect microphone sensitivity?

A well-specified ePTFE vent preserves sensitivity; only a tiny, predictable loss is introduced.

How do I choose the right acoustic membrane for my device?

The right choice depends on three constraints: your audio budget (allowed insertion loss), the enclosure’s IP rating, and the assembly method. Choose ePTFE when you need ≤1 dB loss plus a wide temperature range (−40 °C to +260 °C); choose TPU for lower cost and higher flexibility in mild environments; choose PU for the tightest budgets. Send us your drawing and we propose the grade.

Can one acoustic membrane serve both speaker and microphone ports?

Yes — the same acoustic-vent family covers both. Microphone ports are more sensitivity-critical, so they receive a tighter insertion-loss spec (≤1 dB), while speaker ports can tolerate slightly more. We tune pore size, thickness, and active area per port rather than using a one-size disc.
