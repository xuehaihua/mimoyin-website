Acoustic Vent Membrane

Waterproof Vent Membrane for Outdoor Electronics | IP67 IP68

Why Outdoor Electronics Need a Breathable Vent

Rugged speakers, GPS units, action cameras, and phones live in rain, spray, sand, and thermal shock. Four failure modes dominate field returns:

  • Speaker cone waterlogging—a sealed "waterproof" box traps water against the cone, killing bass.
  • Housing fogging from condensation after a cold-to-warm swing.
  • Dust and sand clogging of mic and port meshes.
  • Thermal breath stress—sealed bodies flex and crack through day-night temperature cycles.

A correctly specified ePTFE vent is the water barrier, the sound window, and the pressure path in one part.

Key Features and Performance

  • Acoustic Transparency: insertion loss ≤1 dB at 1 kHz (ePTFE); premium acoustic-grade PU film—such as the Japanese film we tested—reaches 0.5–1.5 dB, in the same league as ePTFE. Non-fluorine film sits at 6–10 dB. Material choice therefore hinges on waterproof depth and chemical/thermal stability, not acoustic loss alone. Where ePTFE pulls clearly ahead: it reaches 2–50 m waterproof depth and a −40 °C to +260 °C thermal range—premium PU tops out near 1–2 m and a far lower temperature ceiling.
  • Waterproof Depth: ePTFE vents are rated from 2 m (IP68) up to 10 m, 30 m, and 50 m for submersion use; PU film typically caps at 1–2 m.
  • Air Permeability: ≥700 ml/min @ Ø10 mm orifice for ePTFE, drying the chamber after spray.
  • Temperature Range: ePTFE operates −40 °C to +260 °C; PU and TPU hit a much lower thermal ceiling and harden or creep sooner.
  • Adhesive Backing: 3M 9448A / 9495LE PSA, rated −40 °C to +90 °C.
  • Thickness & Tolerance: 0.05–0.20 mm film, die-cut to ±0.05 mm for small ports.

How to Select the Right Vent for Outdoor Gear

Four pitfalls cause most rugged-device field failures:

  1. Sealed but not breathable. A box that can't vent traps condensation; size airflow to ≥700 ml/min @ Ø10 mm orifice.
  2. Unstated acoustic loss. Ask for ≤1.5 dB at 1 kHz; low-grade films—thick PU or non-fluorine—are what muffle the speaker.
  3. Backing adhesive out of temp range. Desert gear hits 70 °C; generic PSA lifts. Specify 3M outdoor PSA.
  4. No dust/IP proof. Beach sand clogs cheap meshes; ePTFE's hydrophobic layer sheds it.

FAQ

Will a waterproof membrane block sound on my speaker?
Not with ePTFE. A correctly specified ePTFE acoustic vent adds ≤1 dB insertion loss at 1 kHz—imperceptible—while blocking water and dust. Low-grade non-fluorine films are what add 6–10 dB and muffle sound; by contrast, acoustic-grade PU films measure only 0.5–1.5 dB — in the same league as ePTFE (≤1 dB). Specify an acoustic-grade membrane to stay ≤1.5 dB. For designs needing >2 m depth or high heat, ePTFE is the only film that holds up.

IP67 vs IP68 for outdoor gear?
IP67 (1 m / 30 min) covers rain and splash; IP68 (2 m / 30 min) adds submersion for kayak, pool, and beach use. Most rugged outdoor gear now targets IP68.

Can you die-cut custom shapes?
Yes. We laser-cut ePTFE vents to your port geometry at ±0.05 mm and supply free samples plus a full acoustic + waterproof test report per lot.
