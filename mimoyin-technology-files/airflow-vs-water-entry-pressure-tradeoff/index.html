How to Balance Airflow and Water Entry Pressure in ePTFE Vent Membranes

Figures are schematic illustrations only; they are not photographs of products or measured data.

Scope: this article is about the membrane material used behind a vent, not a finished valve or vent-pad assembly. Values are material-level reference ranges; the final grade is matched to the customer's drawing.

1. Why the two targets pull against each other

A waterproof breathable membrane has one job that sounds like a contradiction: let gas pass, keep liquid water out. Both behaviours come from the same microporous structure. Pore size and open porosity decide how easily air molecules diffuse through, and they also decide how much pressure it takes to force water into and through those pores.

Open the structure up and airflow rises, but the water entry pressure drops. Tighten it and water resistance improves, but pressure equalisation slows down. There is no single membrane that is best at both ends, which is why every real specification is a compromise tied to one enclosure and one exposure condition.

Three variables do most of the work:

  • Pore size and distribution — larger, more uniform pores raise flow but lower the threshold at which water is pressed through.
  • Open porosity and thickness — more open area per unit thickness raises flow; a thicker film adds resistance to both gas and liquid.
  • Surface energy — the inherently low surface energy of ePTFE is what makes the pores hydrophobic, so a pore that is large enough to pass gas still resists wetting.

2. Which target comes first, by application

The right balance depends on what the enclosure actually sees. Use these rules as a starting point, then verify against the real test conditions:

Application conditionPrioritiseReasoning
Fast pressure swings (sealed housings under thermal cycling)AirflowGas must equalise quickly or the housing bulges or collapses.
Standing water, pressure washing or splash exposureWater entry pressureLiquid water under pressure is the dominant failure mode.
Microphone / speaker openingsAcoustic loss together with airflowSound must pass with minimal loss while the port stays breathable.
Salt mist, dust or oil exposureFace protection and grade marginContaminants slowly block the exposed face and drift airflow.

IP67/IP68 is a result of the whole enclosure, not a membrane rating. Housing seal, gasket compression and adhesive bonding affect the final result as much as the film itself.

3. Put both targets on one test basis

Airflow is only meaningful when the test conditions are stated. Our material-level basis is ml/min at a Ø10 mm orifice under 7 kPa differential pressure — the total flow through a fixed test port, not a per-area value. Ø10 mm is the test tool aperture, not a customer product dimension.

Water entry pressure is reported as a material-level water column, matched to the worst-case exposure. When you compare two suppliers, make sure both numbers use the same orifice, pressure and reporting method. A per-area flux value and a fixed-orifice total flow differ by orders of magnitude and must never be placed side by side as if they were the same unit.

FAQ

Why does higher airflow usually mean lower water entry pressure?

Both come from the same microporous structure. Larger pores or higher porosity pass gas faster but let water through at a lower pressure, so the two targets move against each other.

What test basis should I use to compare airflow?

State the pressure and orifice, for example ml/min at a Ø10 mm orifice under 7 kPa. A number without both is not comparable between suppliers.
