Why ePTFE Vent Membrane Airflow Requires Fixed Ø10 mm and 7 kPa Test Standards

Figures are schematic illustrations only; they are not photographs of products or measured data.

Scope: the figures discussed here are material-level test values for membrane supplied by Mimoyin, used behind a vent or inside a die-cut part. They are not finished-assembly measurements.

1. The common mistake: a number with no conditions

An airflow figure quoted as just "5,000 ml/min" tells you almost nothing. Flow through a porous film depends on how big the test opening is, how much pressure pushes the air through, the film area, thickness and temperature. Change any of those and the number changes — often by a lot.

The most frequent sourcing error is comparing figures measured in different ways as if they were the same unit. Two formats cause most of the confusion:

  • Per-area flux — written as ml/min/cm² (or ml/cm²/min), the flow through one square centimetre of film.
  • Fixed-orifice total flow — written as ml/min at a stated orifice and pressure, the total flow through the whole test port.

These are different physical quantities. A per-area value and a fixed-orifice total flow differ by orders of magnitude; there is no honest way to place them in the same column as if they were directly comparable.

2. The three elements that must be stated

A comparable airflow figure carries three pieces of information. Our material-level basis is ml/min @ Ø10 mm @ 7 kPa.

Fixed area — what Ø10 mm means

Ø10 mm is the diameter of the test aperture. It fixes the test area so results from different labs mean the same thing. It is a property of the test rig, not of the customer's product: a vent pad in an enclosure may be far smaller or larger than 10 mm. Confusing the test orifice with the product size is a common source of wrong assumptions.

Fixed pressure — what 7 kPa means

7 kPa (70 mbar) is the differential pressure applied across the film during the test. Flow rises with pressure, so a value measured at a different pressure is a different result. Stating the pressure lets a buyer judge whether the figure matches the real pressure swings the enclosure will see.

Total flow — from ml/min to a die-cut part

ml/min reports the total volume of air passing through the full test port per minute. For a die-cut part, flow scales with the usable membrane area and the part geometry, so the final part airflow is matched per drawing and verified on the customer's interface. The material figure is a starting reference, not a finished-part result.

3. A supplier-data checklist for procurement

When two datasheets land on your desk, run through these questions before comparing anything:

  1. Is the orifice diameter stated? If not, the area is unknown.
  2. Is the differential pressure stated? If not, the driving force is unknown.
  3. Is the unit per-area flux or fixed-orifice total flow? Do not mix them.
  4. Is the value a material-level figure or a finished-part measurement?
  5. Are water entry pressure, adhesive temperature limit and film temperature reported separately?
  6. Is there lot-level test data, and is documentation available on request?

FAQ

Why can't I compare ml/min/cm2 with ml/min?

They are different quantities: flow per unit area versus total flow through a fixed port. They differ by orders of magnitude and cannot be compared directly.

What does Ø10 mm mean in an airflow figure?

The diameter of the test aperture, not a product dimension. Stating it fixes the test area so figures from different labs are comparable.
