What Is an ePTFE Waterproof Breathable Membrane?

Expanded PTFE (ePTFE) is produced by stretching PTFE into a node-and-fibril microstructure with billions of pores per square centimeter. The pore size (typically 0.1–5 µm) is small enough to stop water droplets and dust, yet large enough for water vapor and air molecules to diffuse. This is what gives the membrane both waterproof and breathable behavior at the same time.

Material Composition & Structure

The membrane is supplied as a bare ePTFE film or laminated to a supporting substrate (PET, PP, or non-woven) for die-cutting and bonding. A pressure-sensitive adhesive (PSA) backing is optional for direct mounting onto enclosures. Pore structure is controlled during biaxial stretching, which determines the final air permeability and water entry pressure.

Key Performance Advantages

  • True two-way breathability — air and vapor pass, liquid stays out.
  • Wide temperature range — stable from -40 °C to +260 °C, far beyond PU or non-fluorine films (which cap near 120 °C).
  • High water column — 2–50 m depth rating versus only 1–2 m for PU / non-fluorine membranes.
  • Chemical inertness — resists oils, solvents, and cleaning agents used in automotive and industrial environments.

Typical Application Scenarios

  • Automotive ECU, sensors, BMS, and headlight housings
  • TWS earbuds, speakers, and microphones (acoustic grade)
  • Outdoor and wearable electronics
  • Medical device vents and sterile enclosures
  • Industrial sensor and control boxes

How to Choose the Right Specification

Selection depends on three variables: (1) required air permeability for pressure equalization speed, (2) minimum water column for the installation environment, and (3) temperature and chemical exposure. For submerged or wash-down parts, specify the 30–50 m water-column grade. For acoustic parts, switch to the acoustic-grade film with ≤ 1 dB insertion loss.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does "breathable" mean for a waterproof membrane?

It means air and water vapor can diffuse through the micropores while liquid water cannot, allowing internal pressure to equalize without letting water in.

What IP rating can ePTFE membranes achieve?

Properly specified ePTFE vents reach IP67 and IP68, protecting against temporary immersion and continuous submersion respectively.

How deep can it be submerged?

Depending on grade, ePTFE membranes withstand 2–50 meters of water column — far deeper than PU or non-fluorine films limited to 1–2 m.

What is the operating temperature range?

-40 °C to +260 °C continuous. PU and non-fluorine membranes typically fail above 120 °C.

Can it resist chemicals and solvents?

Yes. ePTFE is chemically inert against most acids, alkalis, and solvents, making it suitable for automotive and industrial use.

What unit is air permeability measured in?

Air permeability (透气量) is measured in ml/min @ Ø10 mm orifice at a defined pressure drop (commonly 7 kPa).

Is the membrane customizable?

Yes. Thickness, pore size, shape, adhesive, and air-flow rate can be customized to your test standard and enclosure.

Does it affect sound transmission?

Standard ePTFE slightly attenuates sound. For audio parts use the acoustic-grade film with ≤ 1 dB insertion loss.

How is it mounted?

By die-cutting and bonding with PSA backing, ultrasonic welding, or heat lamination onto the enclosure vent.

What certifications apply?

RoHS and REACH compliance; material properties verified per ASTM and IEC test methods listed above.
