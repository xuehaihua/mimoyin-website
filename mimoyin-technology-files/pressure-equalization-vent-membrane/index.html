Why Pressure Equalization Matters

Sealed enclosures act like balloons: as temperature or altitude shifts, internal air expands or contracts, creating pressure that bulges housings, stresses gaskets, and draws in moisture through micro-leaks. A vent membrane equalizes that pressure passively, so the seal only has to block liquid—not pressure.

Common Failure Modes It Prevents

  1. Enclosure bulging or suction deformation from pressure delta.
  2. Condensation inside the housing as warm humid air cools.
  3. Seal and gasket premature failure under repeated pressure cycling.
  4. Battery outgassing in new-energy packs needing safe venting.

How to Select the Right Pressure Equalization Vent

  1. Calculate required air permeability from enclosure volume and expected ΔT/Δaltitude.
  2. Select installation method: adhesive, snap-in, or welded.
  3. Specify membrane size and shape to the available port.
  4. Confirm IP rating (IP67 splash vs IP68 immersion).

How to Calculate the Required Airflow

Select the airflow grade that keeps the pressure differential inside the membrane's safe range. A practical starting point for a sealed enclosure:

  1. Estimate free internal air volume V (cm³).
  2. Estimate the worst-case temperature swing ΔT (°C) the device experiences.
  3. Set a target equalization time t (seconds) — shorter is safer for seals and gaskets.
  4. Required airflow ≈ V × ΔT ÷ (273 + Tavg) ÷ t, then choose the next higher ePTFE grade.

Example: a 200 cm³ automotive ECU housing that swings 60 °C in 10 s needs roughly 200 × 60 ÷ 303 ÷ 10 ≈ 4 ml/min minimum. A mid-grade ePTFE vent (1,000 – 5,000 ml/min) gives a large safety margin and faster transient response.

Pressure Equalization Vent Selection Checklist

  • Define the enclosure free volume and worst-case ΔT.
  • Pick ePTFE when temperature, chemical, or depth rating exceeds TPU/PU limits.
  • Match the vent aperture to the housing wall thickness and adhesive area.
  • Confirm IP67/IP68 on the assembled housing, not the bare film.
  • Request a free die-cut sample for validation before mass production.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will the vent membrane clog over time?

ePTFE is hydrophobic and self-cleaning; under normal conditions it stays permeable for the device's life.

Can the vent be installed on a moving or vibrating enclosure?

Yes—adhesive and welded mounts tolerate vibration; specify the duty in your RFQ.

Does the vent affect EMI/RF shielding?

No. The film is dielectric and does not compromise shielding.

What is the typical service life?

ePTFE vents are rated for the operating life of the enclosure under specified conditions (-40°C to +260°C).

Can these vents be used in medical or food equipment?

Standard grades are industrial; notify us for medical/food-grade material requests.
